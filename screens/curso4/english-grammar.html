🕐 Telling Time

o'clock, half past...
We ask What time is it? and answer It's.... In English we usually say the minutes first, then the hour.

🇪🇸 En español: preguntamos la hora con What time is it? y respondemos con It's.... Normalmente decimos los minutos antes que la hora.

🕔 Minutes Past / To

💡 Truco: si los minutos son 1-30, usamos past (después de la hora). Si son 31-59, usamos to (contamos hacia la hora siguiente): 5:40 → twenty to six, no "forty past five".

❓ Wh- Questions

What, where, when...
We use wh- words to ask for specific information: things, places, time, people, reasons and how.

🇪🇸 En español: usamos las wh- words para pedir información concreta: cosas, lugares, tiempo, personas, razones y modo.
❓ The Six Wh- Words
Word
Asks about
Example
What
things / actions
What is your name? My name is Ana.
Where
places
Where do you live? I live in Madrid.
When
time
When is your birthday? It's in June.
Who
people
Who is your teacher? Mr Smith.
Why
reasons
Why are you happy? Because it's Friday.
How
manner / way
How do you go to school? By bus.
❓ More Wh- Words

💡 Order: wh- word + do/does/is/are + subject + verb...? Example: Where do you live?

🟠 Could

Past ability, polite requests
Could is the past of can. We use it to talk about ability in the past, and also to make polite requests. Could is the same for every subject.

🇪🇸 En español: could es el pasado de can. Lo usamos para hablar de habilidad en el pasado, y también para pedir cosas educadamente.
✅ Past Ability
Subject
Example
I
could swim when I was five.
She
could read at the age of four.
They
could not come to the party.
🙏 Polite Requests
Subject
Example
You
Could you help me, please?
You
Could I have some water, please?
❓ Interrogative
Subject
Example
you
Could you swim when you were five?
she
Could she read at the age of four?
they
Could they come to the party?
💡 Contraction: could not → couldn't. Could you...? is more polite than Can you...?

🔴 Must

Strong obligation
We use must for strong obligation and rules — things that are very important to do. Must not (mustn't) means something is forbidden. Must is the same for every subject and has no past form.

🇪🇸 En español: usamos must para obligación fuerte y normas. Must not significa que algo está prohibido.
✅ Obligation
Subject
Example
You
must wear a seatbelt in the car.
Students
must do their homework.
❌ Prohibition (mustn't)
Subject
Example
You
must not talk during the exam.
You
must not run in the corridor.
❓ Interrogative
Subject
Example
I
Must I finish this today?
we
Must we wear a uniform?
💡 Contraction: must not → mustn't. For the past, we use had to instead of must.

🇪🇸 Ojo: preguntar con Must...? es poco habitual en inglés — normalmente se pregunta con Do I have to...? en su lugar. Aun así, Must...? es correcto y lo verás en exámenes.

🟡 Have To

Obligation from outside
Have to also means obligation, but it comes from outside (rules, other people), not from the speaker. He/She/It uses has to.

🇪🇸 En español: have to también significa obligación, pero viene de fuera (normas, otras personas), no de quien habla. He/She/It usa has to.
✅ Affirmative
Subject
Example
I
have to wear a uniform at school.
He
has to finish his homework tonight.
❌ Negative (don't/doesn't have to)
Subject
Example
I
don't have to get up early on Sunday.
She
doesn't have to cook today.
❓ Interrogative
Do/Does
Example
Do
I have to bring my book?
Does
he have to wear a tie?
💡 Careful! Don't have to = it's not necessary (you can choose). Mustn't = it's forbidden (you can't).

🟢 -er / -est

Comparatives & superlatives
We use -er to compare two things (comparative), and -est to compare three or more (superlative, always with the).

🇪🇸 En español: usamos -er para comparar dos cosas, y -est para comparar tres o más (siempre con the).

🆚 Comparing Two Things (than)

💡 Spelling: nice → nicer (drop the e) · big → bigger (double the last letter) · happy → happier (y → i)

🇪🇸 -er + than siempre van juntos al comparar dos cosas: "taller than" (más alto que). Con adjetivos largos (interesting, beautiful...) no añadimos -er, usamos more + adjective + than.

🟤 Adverbs

sometimes, often...
Adverbs of frequency tell us how often something happens. They usually go before the main verb, but after the verb to be.

🇪🇸 En español: los adverbios de frecuencia dicen con qué frecuencia pasa algo. Normalmente van antes del verbo principal, pero después de to be.
📊 The Frequency Scale
Adverb
%
Example
always
100%
I always brush my teeth.
usually
90%

often
70%
We often play football.
sometimes
50%

rarely
10%

never
0%
I never go to bed late.
📍 Where Does It Go?
Rule
Example
Before the verb
I always do my homework.
After to be
She is never late.
💡 Never say "never" with a negative verb — never is already negative: I never go, not "I don't never go".

🔵 Prepositions

over, into, out of...
Prepositions of place tell us where something is. Prepositions of movement tell us how something moves from one place to another.

🇪🇸 En español: las de lugar dicen dónde está algo. Las de movimiento dicen cómo se mueve algo de un sitio a otro.
= la cosa = el objeto de referencia = movimiento
